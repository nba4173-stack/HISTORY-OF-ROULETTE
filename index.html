02 / People & origins

From a curious experiment to Monte Carlo glamour.

Roulette's history is a mix of scientific curiosity, European gaming culture, and the rise of Monaco as a casino destination.

1623–1662 · France

Blaise Pascal

The mathematician and inventor experimented with perpetual-motion machines in the 17th century. His work is often mentioned in roulette's origin story, though the modern game developed later.

1806–1877 · France / Monaco

François Blanc

Known as the “Magician of Monte Carlo,” Blanc helped establish the Monte Carlo casino in the 19th century and was central to Monaco's rise as a gaming destination.

1818–1889 · Monaco

Prince Charles III

Monaco's ruler during the casino's development, Charles III supported the transformation of the principality's economy. The Monte Carlo district takes its name from him.

04 / Game basics

How a round works.

A quick, beginner-friendly overview of a standard European roulette round.

THE OBJECTIVE

Predict where the ball will land.

A dealer spins the wheel in one direction and sends a small ball around the track in the opposite direction. When the ball settles into a numbered pocket, bets are resolved.

European wheel: 37 pockets — numbers 1–36 plus a single green 0.
The house edge on standard European roulette bets is about 2.70%.
1

Choose a bet

Pick a single number or a broader option such as red/black, odd/even, low/high, or a group of numbers.

2

The wheel spins

The dealer spins the wheel and releases the ball. In a real casino, betting closes before the result is announced.

3

Find the winning pocket

The ball lands in one numbered pocket. The dealer identifies the result and winning bets are paid according to the rules.

4

Understand the odds

A straight-up bet on one number pays 35 to 1, but the chance of hitting that number on a European wheel is 1 in 37.
